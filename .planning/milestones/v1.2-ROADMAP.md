## Overview

v1.1 closed all nine limitations the v1.0 README listed — and left **seven** behind it. An
investigation on 2026-08-13 sorted those seven into four that could close without a
successor limitation and three that could not close honestly and should instead get a
record. The milestone's acceptance bar was one sentence: **no new bullet is born.**

It met that bar. The README's Limitations section went from seven bullets to three, the four
closures **deleted** rather than reworded into the list, and each of the three survivors now
ends at an ADR or an operations note that argues it.

But the milestone's real character came from something the plan did not contain. One phase
spent real money on real answers, and that single paid run found three things no free test
could see — a mislabelling classifier, a truncating judge, and two grading paths that
disagree with each other. Two of the seven phases exist only because of what it found or
what a user reported. **Three of this milestone's most valuable findings arrived from
outside its own plan**, which is the pattern worth carrying forward more than any of the
individual fixes.

## Phases

- **18 — Independent eval judge.** The judge moved off the critic's model to
  `claude-opus-4-8` (ADR-0012, superseding ADR-0010 and deliberately reopening the reversal
  register v1.1 had closed as spent). Its response handling now checks `stop_reason` before
  reading content, because a safety-classifier refusal had been surfacing as a misleading
  parse error.
- **19 — Credential validity, log addressability, demo CSP.** `/health` reports whether keys
  actually *work* beside whether they are present, from a cached probe the endpoint never
  waits on — so a provider outage still cannot restart a healthy container. `run_finished`
  carries `session_id`. The demo page ships a CSP derived from the page itself. The phase
  found that a failed *streaming* run had been logging nothing at all: every failed demo
  run, invisible.
- **20 — Note count bound.** A per-owner cap with oldest-first eviction, identical across
  four backends. Tie-breaking was the hard part — `time.time()` measured 14 unique values
  per 200 calls — so each backend uses a storage-native secondary key. Mutation testing
  found the shared contract suite *structurally blind* to one chroma regression; a dedicated
  gate covers what the suite cannot.
- **21 — Forty recorded answers.** The paid record run: five operator-approved stages,
  **$9.9019 against a $17.4812 quote**. Mid-run it measured the requirement's own two halves
  pulling against each other and the criterion was renegotiated with evidence (below).
- **21.5 — Classifier on its own model (INSERTED).** Phase 21's refusals exposed the
  classifier mislabelling a whole stratum. A probe scored Opus 5 at **37/38 against Sonnet
  5's 32/38** — five fixes, zero regressions, +0.2% a run — and the switch was gated on
  repeating the measurement before trusting it (ADR-0013).
- **22 — Limitations recorded.** The close-out: four bullets deleted and verified on the git
  axis, three survivors linked to records, ADR-0014 stating cost-approximation-by-design with
  four reasons Admin-API reconciliation was rejected, and an intro that says what remains is
  chosen, recorded and argued for.
- **22.5 — The demo shows progress (INSERTED, hotfix).** A user reported the demo looking
  frozen. It was not broken: the page could only draw a stage when one *finished*, and the
  researcher takes ~120 seconds. The supervisor already announced what it was routing to and
  the stream was discarding it as "pure noise on the wire".

## What the milestone is actually about

**The criterion that was renegotiated with evidence.** REQ-forty-recorded-answers said all
forty cases carry a recorded answer *and* that a committed fixture is one the graders and
judge approved. On the real pipeline those are incompatible: 15 of 40 satisfy the second
only by failing the first. The ratified criterion became **recorded or documented refusal**,
the union held total by a test, with `--force` refused outright — buying the number forty by
stamping fixtures `forced` would have discarded the property that makes a fixture worth
grading. **The literal original wording was never met, and the record says so.**

**Three findings free testing could not reach.** The keyless suite stubs the classifier and
the critic, so drift between the shipped pipeline and what the golden cases assert is
invisible to it by construction. Eight refusals named one identical mismatch. Two more were
a judge-harness defect — verdicts truncating against a token budget shared with adaptive
thinking, exactly as `graders.py`'s own comment had predicted. Six were fixtures that passed
record-time grading and then failed replay, because `grade_case_pins` runs only on the replay
path.

**Two hotfixes from outside the roadmap.** 21.5 and 22.5 join 10.5 and 17.5: the pattern of
live problems arriving from somewhere other than the plan now has four instances across three
milestones.

**Deriving beat freezing, measurably.** Phase 19 chose to derive the CSP hashes from the page
at runtime rather than freeze literals. Two later phases then edited that page. Under the
rejected design, both edits would have blocked the live demo's own JavaScript with the test
suite green.

## Carried forward

- The judge's truncating token budget — recorded as a defect, not fixed.
- The five contested cases' `case_pins`, whose re-authoring was tried and **reverted**: the
  same pins must also satisfy each case's hand-authored reference report, so every
  replacement tested less than what it replaced. `dataset.py` ended untouched.
- The suite-count literal is ungated in three files; the derived-counts gate covers eval
  counts only.
- The note cap cannot collide with eval seeding by a margin (1 note vs a cap of 100) rather
  than by a gate.
- `.planning/codebase/CONCERNS.md`'s stale no-eviction claim — dispositioned, owned by a
  `/gsd-map-codebase` re-run.
- The production image is not reproducible: a floating `python:3.14-slim` base and unpinned
  transitive dependencies.

## The close-out found a gap in itself

Running `/gsd:complete-milestone` revealed that Phase 21 — the most expensive phase — had no
execution record for its two paid plans, no verification, and an unreconciled validation
contract, while the ROADMAP had both plans ticked. The work had genuinely shipped; **the tick
was ahead of the evidence.** The summaries were reconstructed from archived report JSONs and
labelled as reconstructions, both missing verifications ran retrospectively, and each found
real errors — including four in the reconstructions themselves. Phase 21's promised
settled-judge gate turned out never to have been written, and a planted stale judge passed
all 834 tests until it was.

Archiving a milestone named *"nothing uncovered"* with its most expensive phase unrecorded
would have been the one thing its own argument could not survive.
